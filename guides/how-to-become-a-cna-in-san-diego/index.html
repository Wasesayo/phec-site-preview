The Path

Four steps, start to CNA license.

1. Complete a CDPH-approved training program

State law requires at least 60 hours of classroom instruction and 100 hours of supervised clinical training at a CDPH-approved Nurse Assistant Training Program (NATP). Programs range from accelerated month-long formats to part-time schedules spread over several weeks.

2. Pass your school's final exams

Before you can sit for the state exam, your training program tests you on both theory (written) and skills (hands-on). This is where a program with an open skills lab and instructors who give real feedback makes the biggest difference.

3. Take the CA State Competency Exam

The state exam has a written and a skills portion, administered at an approved testing site. Some schools double as their own testing site, which means you test in the same rooms you trained in instead of traveling somewhere unfamiliar.

4. Receive your CNA license from CDPH

Once you pass, the California Department of Public Health issues your Certified Nursing Assistant license. You're now eligible to work in skilled nursing facilities, hospitals, home health, and hospice settings across the state.

The Real Timeline

How long this actually takes.

Program length varies more than most people expect. An accelerated program like PHEC's runs 22 class days (8 days of theory, 14 days of clinical), offered both as a Monday–Friday weekday session and a Saturday–Sunday weekend session. Part-time and community-college programs can take anywhere from six weeks to a full semester, trading a slower pace for a lower (sometimes free) sticker price. Either way, budget a little extra time after your final class for scheduling and sitting the state exam itself.

Free vs. Paid

Are there free CNA classes in San Diego?

Yes, and it's worth knowing the honest tradeoff before you choose. Some community colleges and adult education programs in San Diego offer no-cost CNA training, sometimes in partnership with a healthcare employer. In exchange, these programs typically come with longer waitlists, a slower part-time pace spread over months instead of weeks, and sometimes a work commitment to the sponsoring employer after graduation.

A paid, accelerated private program trades that wait for speed and a fixed, predictable schedule. Neither path is wrong; it depends on whether you're optimizing for cost or for how fast you can start working. If speed matters and you want one flat price with nothing added later, that's exactly what PHEC's 22-day program is built for.

CNA vs. HHA

Not sure which certification you need?

CNA (Certified Nursing Assistant)

Trained and licensed to provide hands-on care in clinical settings: hospitals, skilled nursing facilities, and long-term care. Requires the full state training program and the CA State Competency Exam. It's also the standard first step if nursing school (LVN or RN) is the longer-term goal.

HHA (Home Health Aide)

Builds on an existing CNA license to provide personal assistance and basic nursing care in a patient's home — through a home health agency or in a hospice setting — rather than a facility. PHEC's HHA program is 5 class days for CNAs looking to add this certification, and it counts toward CNA renewal CEU hours too.

Short version: CNA is the foundational license almost everyone starts with. HHA is an add-on for CNAs who specifically want to work in home care.
